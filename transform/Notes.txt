1. The Core Concept Scaling and Translation
Instead of breaking a signal down
into infinite sine waves (as in Fourier analysis), the CWT breaks the signal
down into shifted and scaled versions of a single, finite, localized
wave—called a Mother Wavelet.The analysis operates on two primary mechanical
actionsTranslation (τ) Shifting the wavelet along the time axis to
localize the analysis to a specific moment.Scaling ($s$) Stretching or
compressing the wavelet.Compressed wavelets (low scale) capture
high-frequency, short-duration events (like sharp spikes).Stretched wavelets
(high scale) capture low-frequency, long-duration events (like decadal
trends or long-period Rossby waves).By systematically translating the
wavelet across the timeseries at many different scales, you calculate how
well the wavelet "matches" the signal at every combination of time and
scale.
